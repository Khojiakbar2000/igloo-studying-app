# Pip chair performance

## Identity lock

Keep Pip's navy feather silhouette, cream face and belly, brown eyes, orange beak and feet, teal braided scarf, dark blue-gray contour, and soft textured storybook shading. Do not change proportions or costume.

## Beat map

- **Notice:** gaze shifts before the head; body remains planted.
- **Waddle:** two alternating contacts; torso leans with the supporting foot; flippers counter-swing and scarf follows.
- **Brake and load:** feet plant, body lowers, gaze fixes on the chair.
- **Hop:** push-off, short airborne arc, scarf lags.
- **Landing and settle:** hips meet the seat, body compresses, flippers finish their arc, Pip settles and brightens.

## Runtime

14 drawings at 12 fps with authored holds, one-shot. Frames 11–13 are new ImageGen chair-specific contact, settling, and seated-hold drawings. The art holds the same head and overall height; compression comes from the pose itself. Character root scale is constant through the whole action. The personal-room action holds the drawn final pose to avoid a shape pop into the limited body/head Rive rig. Study Together retains the existing Rive seated loop.

## Review standard

The browser review clip is `pip-chair-performance-review.webm`: 33 screenshots sampled from the running 430 × 932 app at 12 fps, encoded to WebM. Its sampled filmstrip was inspected for face/scarf continuity, floor contact, travel toward the chair, anticipation, airborne separation, landing compression, and final seat alignment. The action stays within the illustration card and the final drawn pose remains seated on the chair.

This is the benchmark interaction only. The old Rive rig remains limited to two bones and does not provide the independent face, flipper, or foot controls needed for a fully articulated climb. Do not describe this sequence as a fully rigged Rive performance; it is a drawn-pose sequence under the reusable action controller.
